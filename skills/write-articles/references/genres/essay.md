# Essay

**Promise**: a new way to see a problem, argued well enough that a skeptical reader updates.

## Shape

1. **Hook**: a person, a date, a scene, or a confession, kept short.
2. **The received view**, stated fairly.
3. **The turn**: a short pivot sentence on its own line.
4. **The argument in steps**, each anchored on a dated fact, a named system, or a concrete scene from the author's work.
5. **The strongest objection**, named and answered.
6. **What follows**: what the reader should do, build, or expect.
7. **A short closing line** that lands the thesis.

A position paper (a company stating its policy and calling on its industry) adds two parts after the argument: the policy itself in plain sentences the reader could hold the company to, and the standards it proposes to everyone, each with how it is enforced. Model: Afterquery, [A Call for Responsible Training Data Production](https://www.afterquery.com/blog/a-call-for-responsible-training-data-production).

## Dials

- **Length**: 900 to 2,000 words.
- **Voice**: strongly first person for a personal essay; institutional "we" for a position paper.
- **Humor**: welcome in a personal essay about how people work; none in a policy statement.
- **Visuals**: few or none; a screenshot or short clip only when it shows the practice argued for.
- **Code**: none.

## What to study in the models

- Zed, [Xanadu Was Waiting for Agents](https://zed.dev/blog/agentic-xanadu): five paragraphs of history, a three-word pivot, then a list of enabling technologies each with its year and a bold one-line epithet; the predecessor's failure turned into a design lesson; a short closing line.
- Delta, [Please Stop Sending Me Google Docs](https://delta.dev/blog/please-stop-sending-me-google-docs): a workflow confession, a teammate named, the reader's discomfort tackled head on in a provocative heading, then a practical way out for the uncomfortable; ends with an action the reader can take tomorrow.
- Afterquery's position paper: the question reframed from "human or synthetic data" to what produced the data and why it should be trusted; a two-sentence policy; three industry standards; a footnote defining the load-bearing term.

## Watch for

- **Every step of the argument rests on something checkable.** Dates, named systems, and scenes from real work carry an essay; adjectives do not.
- **The opposing view at full strength.** Answering a weak version persuades no one who holds the strong one.
- **The hook pays off.** The opening person or scene returns at the end, or the essay states plainly what it taught.

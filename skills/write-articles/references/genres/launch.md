# Launch

**Promise**: what ships, who it is for, and how to use it today.

## Shape

1. **First sentence**: the capability, who gets it, how to get it. No scene, no backstory; Arthur finds narrative hooks on feature announcements tiresome. When readers may not know the product, one clause says what it is.
2. **Why it matters**: one concrete paragraph about what the reader can now do that they could not before, and the problem that made it worth building.
3. **Get started**: exact steps, setting paths, commands, versions, plans, platforms.
4. **One use, end to end**: follow a single concrete use from the first command to the visible result, so the reader sees the mechanism work. For a feature that grants or gates access, the trust model and its edges belong here: who can do what to whom, and what the approval actually covers. A longer technical story belongs in a separate deep dive, linked.
5. **What is not there yet**: known limits and maturity, stated plainly, including "early" when it is.
6. **What comes next**: the concrete next items, without dates you cannot keep.
7. **One action**: try it, update, send feedback through a named channel.

## Dials

- **Length**: 400 to 1,000 words.
- **Voice**: "we", direct, present tense.
- **Humor**: none needed.
- **Visuals**: one screenshot or short clip per key step, captioned with the action shown.
- **Evidence**: exact version numbers, plan names, platform availability, each in the ledger.

## What to study in the model

Delta, [Claude Agent is now in Delta](https://delta.dev/blog/claude-agent-in-delta): two opening sentences that say what ships and on which plans; an admission that it ships earlier than usual because it was the most requested feature; a "What's coming next" list naming three concrete gaps; a protocol section for third-party developers with a reference implementation; the feedback command named in the text.

## Watch for

- **Availability is exact**: who gets it, on which plan, platform, and version, from when.
- **Maturity is honest**: a preview is called a preview.
- **A social post is a different format.** When the user wants the LinkedIn or X version of the launch, that is a short post (what it does, one benefit line, version and link), outside this skill's scope.

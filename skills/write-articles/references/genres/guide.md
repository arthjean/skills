# Guide

**Promise**: after reading, the reader can apply a practice to their own work and knows when it does not apply.

## Shape

1. **Title and dek** that state the promise and the decision the guide helps make.
2. **When to use this, and when not**: the conditions, as a short list. Advice without its boundary reads as dogma.
3. **Vocabulary**: define the few primitives the guide relies on.
4. **The primitives or steps, in the order announced.** For each: the naive approach, how it fails, the recommended pattern, and a short list of practices led by a bold verb.
5. **How it fits together**: one section that reassembles the parts into the whole cycle.
6. **Tools and next action**: what to run or try first, on what.

## Dials

- **Length**: 1,500 to 4,000 words.
- **Voice**: second person, imperative, "we" when reporting what the team learned.
- **Humor**: none needed.
- **Visuals**: a comparison table when options differ on several axes, diagrams of the assembled system.
- **Evidence**: customer quotes only when sourced and attributed by name and company; demo results labeled as demos.

## What to study in the models

- Claude, [Building agents: When to fold subagents into your main agent loop](https://claude.com/resources/articles/building-agents-when-to-fold-subagents-into-your-main-agent-loop): three conditions under which subagents still win, stated before the advice against them; failure modes given memorable names; a migration path one subagent at a time with an eval at each step; a closing principle (measure, change one thing, keep what works).
- Claude, [Building always-on agents](https://claude.com/resources/articles/building-always-on-agents): the title's misconception corrected in the opening; five primitives announced, then treated in that order; one rule of thumb per primitive; a final section that reassembles the cycle.
- Claude, [Using Claude Opus 5.5 as your daily driver](https://claude.com/resources/articles/using-claude-opus-5-5-as-your-daily-driver-for-knowledge-work): a comparison table of models by use, price, and speed; two bold-led lists (tasks to try, habits to change); a worked demo; a closing instruction naming the exact task and setting to try.

## Watch for

- **Each recommendation carries its reason and its boundary.**
- **Steps are executable**: real commands, real setting paths, real file names.
- **The order announced is the order delivered.**

# Incident

**Promise**: an affected reader learns what happened, whether they are affected, and exactly what to do.

## Shape

Use these sections in this order; readers in a hurry scan for them.

1. **Summary**: what happened, when, the scope in numbers, who is affected, and that affected people have been contacted.
2. **What happened**: a dated timeline in plain sentences, from root cause to containment.
3. **What was not affected**: an explicit list, so most readers can stop worrying.
4. **What we did**: the remediations, each concrete.
5. **What you should do**: the exact steps for affected readers, with versions and identifiers; say when a simple uninstall or reset is not enough.
6. **What comes next**: the lasting changes, an apology in plain words, a contact address, and a note that the post will be updated.

## Dials

- **Length**: 800 to 1,600 words.
- **Voice**: signed by the team; plain, factual, calm.
- **Humor**: none.
- **Numbers**: bounded and exact ("at most 5,000 users", "fewer than 0.01% of customers", "about 11 hours"), absolute dates.
- **Visuals**: none, or a timeline.

## What to study in the model

Cursor, [An update on our recent security incident](https://cursor.com/blog/security-incident-update): the four headed sections; a timeline that starts at the vendor where the key leaked; scope bounded in numbers; a long "not affected" list; remediations written as completed actions; the affected versions named exactly; an apology that says they can and must do better.

## Watch for

- **Known and unknown kept apart**: what is confirmed, what is still under investigation, when the next update comes.
- **"You are affected if"** stated as a testable condition the reader can check.
- **Every figure is in the ledger**, sourced from the investigation, and reviewed by whoever owns the response before publication.
